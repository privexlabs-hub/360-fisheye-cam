# User guide

## Start

- **Start camera** turns on the front camera. Your browser asks for permission the first time.
- **Open a photo or video** applies the effect to a file on your device (JPG, PNG, WebP, MP4, MOV, WebM).

Switch between them any time with the **Camera / Files** button at the bottom right, or **Open** at the top.

## Choose a look

The row under the preview picks the background:

- **Tiny planet** is the default. You are cut out of the picture and shown in front of a built-in tiny-planet scene: Sunny street, Beach or Night city (choose in Adjust → Planet scene).
- **My planet image** uses your own picture as the planet. Choose it with the picker that opens, or in Adjust → Planet image. A 2:1 panorama from a 360° camera or a phone panorama app gives the most natural planet. An ordinary photo works too: it is mirrored so the circle closes without a seam.
- You don't need a planet scene: **Real background · planet** removes the cover and curls your actual surroundings into a planet.
- **Real background · fisheye** shows your actual surroundings through an extreme fisheye lens.

The planet looks need the person cut-out. Until it has loaded (a few seconds the first time), the real-background planet is shown.

## Adjust

Tap **Adjust** (on a computer the panel is always on the right). Only the controls for the current look are shown.

| Control | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Planet scene | Built-in scene for the Tiny planet look: Sunny street, Beach or Night city. |
| Planet size / height / rotation | Size, vertical position and spin of the tiny planet. |
| Edge wrap (real planet) | How much of your surroundings wraps around the planet. Higher values pull in more mirrored, blended surroundings. |
| Face above centre (real planet) | How far your face sits above the planet's centre. |
| Subject size / height | How big you appear and where your face sits. The planet looks and the fisheye look each remember their own size and height; in the planet looks your head sits above the planet by default. |
| Fisheye strength | How strongly the virtual lens bends the edges. |
| Lens type | Fisheye (equisolid) is the default; Extreme (orthographic) squeezes the edges hardest. |
| Tilt | Rotates the whole view. |
| Face pop / size | Enlarges the nose and mouth area, like a lens right at your face. Set it to 0 to turn it off. |
| Follow my face | Keeps the effect centred on your face. Turn it off to place it by hand with the position sliders. |
| Camera angle | Your camera's field of view across the short side. Raise it for ultra-wide cameras. |
| Frame | Output shape: same as source, 9:16, 1:1 or 16:9. |
| Quality | Output size, up to 1280 or 1920 pixels on the long side (never larger than the source). |
| Mirror | Flips left and right. On by default for the front camera, off for files. Recordings and photos match what you see. |

**Reset** restores the current look's defaults. Settings are remembered on this device.

## Record and save

- **Live camera:** the red button records with sound (microphone permission is asked the first time); press again to stop. **Photo** takes a still. Recordings can be up to 10 minutes. Where the browser allows, they are written to the app's private storage on your device as you record, so long recordings don't fill the phone's memory; the app warns you if free space is low.
- **Photos from files:** **Save photo**.
- **Videos from files:** **Play** previews with the effect, and **Export video** processes every frame of the original. Export keeps the original timing and passes the sound through unchanged where the browser allows. A progress bar shows how far it has got, and **Cancel** stops it.

After saving you see the result. **Save / Share** opens the share sheet on phones (choose Save Video or Save Image) or downloads the file on computers.

## Tips for the best look while seated

- Hold the phone at arm's length, a little above eye level, and tilt it down. This imitates the selfie-stick angle of real 360 cameras.
- Face the light. Face tracking and the cut-out both work better in good light.
- Keep your whole head inside the frame. Anything at the edge of the camera's view gets mirrored in the real-background planet.
